How the number is worked out

No slicer, no server, no upload. The page reads the triangle mesh directly and does the arithmetic a slicer would do — which is why the estimate appears the moment you drop the file.

Step 1

Measure the mesh

Solid volume comes from the signed volume of every triangle's tetrahedron to the origin; surface area from the cross product of each triangle's edges. Bounding box gives the footprint.

Step 2

Work out what is actually extruded

A print is not solid. Shell is area × walls × line width; the interior left over is multiplied by the infill density. Together that is the extruded volume.

Step 3

Convert to grams and hours

Grams is extruded volume × the material's density. Time comes from volumetric flow — layer × line width × speed — derated for acceleration and travel, plus a per-layer change cost.

Step 4

Price it like a shop

Filament at your RM/kg, machine time at your hourly rate, a setup fee, a failure allowance for reprints, then margin. Minimum order applies last.

Estimates, not slicer output. Real print time depends on geometry, supports, cooling and how the part is oriented on the plate — a tall thin part with the same volume as a flat one will take considerably longer. Treat the figure as a quote to confirm, which is exactly how a shop uses it.
